## Part 2: The Last Page

Marcus's internal posture work and his unfinished "External Threat Landscape" notes are two halves of the same picture: this assessment establishes *what MedDefense actually has and where it is weak* (assets, controls, gaps), while the threat landscape phase he outlined would establish *who is likely to target those specific weaknesses and how*. The internal assessment has to come first because prioritizing threat intelligence without it would mean guessing at relevance — knowing that ransomware groups favor unpatched remote-access points and unsegmented networks matters far more now that we know, in concrete terms, that MedDefense's entire Central network is flat, MFA covers one account, and Westside has no firewall at all. The gap findings here — especially the repeated, undetected compromise pattern on `billing-srv-01` and the near-total absence of detective controls (GAP-001) — point directly at the threat actor category Marcus's notes anticipated: opportunistic ransomware and cryptomining operators who probe broadly for exactly this kind of unmonitored, flat-network environment, making his proposed next phase the logical and now precisely targeted continuation of this work.
